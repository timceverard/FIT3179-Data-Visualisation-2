More koalas than we counted, but not out of the woods

Koalas live in the eucalyptus forests and woodlands of eastern and southeastern Australia. In Queensland, New South Wales and the ACT they are listed as endangered; in Victoria and South Australia they are not. This data visualisation follows nearly half a million sightings and 60,000 rescues to ask where koalas live, how regions compare, what harms them, and how we have responded. "Listed" means a species is on Australia's national threatened species list, which gives it legal protection; "not listed" means its populations are considered secure.

Section 1 · Populations & habitats

Where do koalas live?

Koalas cling to the eucalypt forests of the east and south-east. A sighting is a record of where someone looked as well as where a koala was, so this section also shows who did the records.

1Koala sightings across Australia

This map displays the density of sightings across Australia. Each small square covers about 30 km × 30 km, and darker green means more koala sightings were recorded there.

Almost every sighting sits within a few hundred kilometres of the east coast.

The darkest squares cluster around South East Queensland and the NSW coast. West of South Australia, there are no wild koalas at all.

2Sightings per 1,000 km² by state

This map compares koala sightings in each state, scaled to the state's land area, so big and small states are comparable. For its size, NSW has the most records: about 377 per 1,000 km², against 8 in South Australia.

3Who recorded them?

This chart shows which programs and organisations recorded Australia's koala sightings. The grid has 100 squares; each one is 1% of all sightings. Nearly two in every three records come from one source: the NSW government's BioNet atlas.

Section 2 · Population trends across regions

How do the regions compare over time?

Sighting records show how koala observations have changed across the states since 2000. Both regions record more koalas now than 20 years ago, with the sharpest rise after 2020, but the listed states (QLD, NSW, ACT) record far more for their size than the unlisted states (VIC, SA). However, it is important to note that these charts show where and when koalas are seen, not how many exist.

4Sightings by year and state, 2000–2026

This heatmap shows how many koala sightings were recorded in each state, every year since 2000. NSW records jumped about fivefold in 2023–24, again, more searching, not more koalas.

5Listed (QLD, NSW, ACT) vs Unlisted (VIC, SA)

This chart compares sightings per 1,000 km² in the listed and unlisted states. For their land area, the listed states now record about 12 times as many sightings as the unlisted states.

6When koalas are seen

This chart shows what share of each group's sightings falls in each month. 45% of sightings in the listed states fall in October and November, which must be koala breeding season.

Section 3 · Factors affecting populations

What puts koalas in hospital?

Queensland's koala hospitals have logged nearly 60,000 rescues since 1997, most of them in South East Queensland, where koala habitat meets fast-growing suburbs. Those records reveal the main threats: disease, cars and dogs.

7Why koalas were rescued, 1997–2024

This chart shows why koalas were rescued in Queensland each year from 1997 to 2024, as a share of that year's rescues. Disease is the largest single cause, and its share grew from 29% in 1997 to 53% in 2024.

8Dangerous times of the year

This chart compares the quietest and busiest month of the year for each cause of rescue. Each line joins a cause's quietest month to its busiest month, where a longer line means a bigger seasonal swing; the multiplier defines how many times busier the peak month is.

9Rescue hotspots in South East Queensland

This map shows how many koala rescues came from each council area in South East Queensland. Each circle is one council area, placed where most of its rescues happened. Moreton Bay and Redland alone account for about 58% of rescues with a known council area.

Section 4 · Conservation over time

How has our response changed?

Over more recent years, dedicated koala surveys multiplied and hospitals got better at getting koalas back into the wild where they belong.

10Koala monitoring programs

This chart shows the span of koala records held by the 12 largest recording programs. Each bar runs from the date of a program's oldest koala record to its newest, labelled with it's total records, where dark bars hold records from before 1980. We can see that koala programs started becoming more common as time went on.

11More rescued koalas are surviving

This chart shows the share of rescued koalas released back to the wild, and the share found dead, each year. Recorded releases rose from 15% of rescues in 1997 to 44% in 2024, while koalas found dead fell from 21% to 7%.

Care is improving, but rescue only treats the symptoms.

Better care can't keep up if the underlying causes aren't addressed.
